# Mental Health and Wellbeing Policy

## 1. Purpose

Zuqah Technologies recognises that mental health is as important as physical
health. One in four people experience a mental health condition at some point
in their lives, and work significantly affects wellbeing — positively or
negatively. This policy describes the support available and the expectations
for how the company and its managers respond to mental health concerns.

## 2. Our commitment

Zuqah Technologies commits to:

- Creating a workplace culture where talking about mental health is normal
  and free from stigma
- Training managers to recognise early signs of mental health difficulties
  and respond appropriately
- Providing practical support through tools, time and professional resources
- Treating mental health conditions with the same care and confidentiality
  as physical health conditions
- Not making employment decisions based on a person's mental health history
  unless the condition directly prevents them from doing the role safely

## 3. Support available

### 3.1 Employee Assistance Programme (EAP)

All employees have access to a free, confidential Employee Assistance
Programme. The EAP provides:

- Up to **6 sessions** of professional counselling per year (in person,
  video or phone)
- 24/7 support helpline — available 365 days a year
- Guidance on financial, legal and family concerns, which are common
  sources of stress
- Digital tools and self-help resources

**How to access:** Contact details for the EAP provider are in the HR portal
under **Wellbeing → EAP**. You do not need to tell your manager or People
Operations to access the EAP. Zuqah Technologies does not receive any
information about individual use.

### 3.2 Mental health days

All employees may take up to **2 mental health days per year** without
requiring a medical certificate. These days are in addition to regular sick
leave. Inform your manager that you are taking a mental health day; you do
not need to explain in detail.

Mental health days are intended for moments when you need time to recover
and reset — not for routine personal appointments, which should be taken
as annual leave.

### 3.3 Flexible working during mental health recovery

If you are managing a mental health condition that affects your ability to
work your standard hours or from the standard location, you may discuss a
temporary flexible arrangement with your manager or People Operations. This
may include adjusted hours, reduced workload for a period or a phased return
from sick leave.

### 3.4 Peer support and mental health first aiders

Zuqah Technologies has trained Mental Health First Aiders among its staff.
They are available for informal conversations and to help colleagues access
support. Names and contact details are in the HR portal under
**Wellbeing → Mental Health First Aiders**.

Mental Health First Aiders are not therapists — they provide a first point
of contact and signpost to appropriate professional support.

## 4. Sick leave for mental health

Mental health conditions are treated the same as physical conditions for the
purpose of sick leave. Employees should follow the normal sick leave
notification procedure (see Paid Time Off and Leave Policy). A doctor's
certificate is required after **3 consecutive days** of absence, as for any
other illness.

Long-term absence due to mental health is managed under the same long-term
sickness absence process as physical illness, with support from People
Operations throughout.

## 5. Manager responsibilities

Managers play the most important role in their team's mental wellbeing.
Managers at Zuqah Technologies are expected to:

- **Check in** — hold regular one-to-ones that include a genuine question
  about how the person is doing, not just task status
- **Act early** — if an employee seems to be struggling, address it with
  compassion and not after the situation has deteriorated
- **Listen without judgement** — when an employee discloses a mental health
  difficulty, the manager's role is to listen and ask what support would help,
  not to diagnose or advise
- **Maintain confidentiality** — information shared about a mental health
  condition should be shared only where operationally necessary and only with
  the employee's knowledge
- **Adjust workload if needed** — consider whether deadlines, meeting load or
  ways of working can be temporarily adjusted during a difficult period
- **Not penalise** — taking sick leave or mental health days for mental health
  reasons must not be used negatively in performance or promotion decisions

Managers who are unsure how to respond to a disclosure or a concern should
contact People Operations for guidance.

## 6. Warning signs

The following changes in a colleague or team member may indicate mental
health difficulties and warrant a compassionate check-in:

- Uncharacteristic absence, lateness or changes in work quality
- Withdrawal from team activities or conversations
- Visible signs of distress (tearfulness, irritability, difficulty
  concentrating)
- Talking about feeling overwhelmed, hopeless or worthless
- Noticeable change in energy, motivation or confidence

A check-in is not a diagnosis. "I've noticed you seem quieter than usual
lately — how are you doing?" is enough.

## 7. Reducing work-related stress

The following practices help prevent work-related stress and are expected
from all managers:

- Set realistic deadlines and review workload regularly
- Ensure annual leave is taken and not routinely cancelled or postponed
- Avoid routinely contacting employees outside working hours
- Communicate changes and uncertainty clearly and promptly
- Create an environment where it is safe to say "I can't take this on right
  now"

## 8. Crisis support

If an employee or colleague is in crisis — expressing suicidal thoughts or
appearing at immediate risk — do not leave them alone. Contact emergency
services (112) and notify People Operations or a company First Aider
immediately.

**iCall (India):** 9152987821 — free professional mental health support

**Vandrevala Foundation:** 1860-2662-345 — 24/7 crisis support

## 9. Confidentiality

Information about an employee's mental health will be held confidentially
and shared only with those who need to know. People Operations may share
relevant information with a manager where a workplace adjustment is needed,
but only with the employee's knowledge and, wherever possible, their consent.

Medical information is stored separately from the main HR file with
restricted access.
